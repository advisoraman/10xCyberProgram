01
The GRC Mental Model
Understanding what GRC is, how the three pillars relate, and where you fit
Foundation

GRC in One Paragraph

Governance is about who makes security decisions and how accountability flows. Risk Management is about identifying what can go wrong, how likely it is, and what it costs — then deciding what to do about it. Compliance is about meeting the specific rules set by laws, regulators, and industry standards. All three are interdependent: governance without risk management is guesswork; compliance without governance is theatre.

In practice, a GRC analyst helps organizations understand their obligations, assess their current posture, identify gaps, and build evidence that controls are working. Every deliverable you produce — a risk register, a gap assessment, an audit report — is a structured argument that the organization is managing its security responsibly.

Governance
Structures decisions. Who owns security? What policies exist? How is accountability enforced? Board, CISO, ISO 27001 ISMS scope, policy hierarchy.
Risk Management
Quantifies uncertainty. What assets exist? What threatens them? How likely? What's the impact? What do we do about it? Risk registers, threat models, BCP.
Compliance
Satisfies obligations. What laws, standards, and contracts require us to do? DPDP Act, CERT-In, ISO 27001, PCI-DSS, SEBI guidelines for Indian BFSI.
Audit
Provides evidence. Are the controls we say we have actually working? Independent verification of governance, risk, and compliance claims.

The Three Lines of Defence Model

Most large organizations use this model to assign GRC responsibilities clearly:

  • 1st Line: Business units — own and manage risk day-to-day (IT, Operations)
  • 2nd Line: Risk and Compliance functions — set standards, monitor, report (GRC team, CISO office)
  • 3rd Line: Internal and external audit — independently verify that lines 1 and 2 are working

As a GRC analyst, you typically sit in the 2nd line — you don't operate controls, you define, measure, and report on them.

💡 For Indian job interviews: Big 4 GRC practices (Deloitte, KPMG, EY, PwC), Infosys Cyber, TCS Risk Advisory — all ask you to explain Governance vs Risk vs Compliance with examples. Know this cold. Also study ISO 27001 + DPDP Act as the two most asked frameworks in India interviews.

Security Frameworks Overview
When to use what, and how they relate to each other
Orientation

Why So Many Frameworks?

Each framework was created for a different context. ISO 27001 is a certifiable management system standard for any organization globally. NIST CSF is a voluntary risk management framework from the US government. CIS Controls is a prioritized technical control set. SOC 2 is a US audit standard for SaaS companies. DPDP is Indian law. They're not competing — they complement each other and often overlap ~60–70%.

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 International Standard

What: Certifiable ISMS (Information Security Management System) standard. Clauses 4–10 define mandatory requirements; Annex A has 93 controls.

When to use: When a client or contract demands it; when building enterprise security governance from scratch; most common GRC certification in India.

Cert possible: Yes — ISO 27001 Lead Auditor (LA) or Lead Implementer (LI). Highly valued.

NIST CSF 2.0 US Gov Framework

What: Six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover. Non-prescriptive, outcome-based. Widely used in US; growing adoption in India.

When to use: Risk-based conversations, board-level security posture reporting, mapping to other frameworks. Great companion to ISO 27001.

Cert possible: No formal cert; NIST Cybersecurity Professional (NCSP) available.

CIS Controls v8 Technical Prioritized

What: 18 controls organized into Implementation Groups (IG1/2/3). IG1 is "basic cyber hygiene" — 56 safeguards. Very practical, tool-mapped.

When to use: SMB security baseline, quick wins prioritization. IG1 is the minimum for any organization. Maps well to ISO 27001 Annex A.

Cert possible: CIS SecureSuite membership; no standalone cert.

SOC 2 (AICPA) Audit Standard

What: Trust Service Criteria (TSC): Security, Availability, Confidentiality, Processing Integrity, Privacy. Type I = design; Type II = 6-month operating effectiveness.

When to use: SaaS companies selling to US enterprise customers. Increasingly required by Indian IT companies with US clients.

Cert possible: Not a certification — it's an audit report issued by a CPA firm.

DPDP Act 2023 🇮🇳 Indian Law

What: India's primary data protection law. Governs processing of digital personal data. Data fiduciaries must implement security safeguards, manage consent, notify breaches.

When to use: Any Indian organization processing personal data. Mandatory — not voluntary. Penalties up to ₹250 crore.

Cert possible: No cert yet; DPDP Compliance Auditor role emerging.

PCI-DSS v4.0 Payment Card Industry

What: 12 requirements for organizations that store/process/transmit cardholder data. Mandated by card networks (Visa, Mastercard). 64 requirements in v4.0.

When to use: Banks, payment gateways, fintech, e-commerce. Mandatory for Indian BFSI sector handling card data.

Cert possible: PCI QSA (Qualified Security Assessor) — demanding but well-paid.

ISO 27001:2022 — Full Walkthrough
Clauses 4–10, Annex A controls, ISMS lifecycle
Deep Dive

ISO 27001 Structure

ISO 27001 has two parts: Clauses 4–10 are the mandatory management requirements — every clause must be addressed for certification. Annex A contains 93 controls across 4 themes (Organisational, People, Physical, Technological). You don't implement all 93 — you decide which apply via the Statement of Applicability (SoA).

The standard is built on the Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) cycle. You don't implement security once — you run a continuous improvement loop.

Clause 4 — Context
Understand the organization: internal/external issues, interested parties (regulators, customers), ISMS scope definition. Where does the ISMS apply?
Clause 5 — Leadership
Top management commitment, security policy at board level, CISO/ISO roles and responsibilities. Leadership must demonstrate commitment, not just sign a policy.
Clause 6 — Planning
Risk assessment methodology, risk treatment plan, Statement of Applicability (SoA), security objectives. Core of GRC work.
Clause 7 — Support
Resources, competence, awareness training, communication plan, documented information (policy, records). Everything needed to run the ISMS.
Clause 8 — Operation
Implement the risk treatment plan. Control the processes. Manage information security objectives in day-to-day operations.
Clause 9 — Performance
Monitoring and measurement, internal audit programme, management review. Prove the ISMS is working — metrics, KPIs, audit reports.
Clause 10 — Improvement
Nonconformities, corrective actions, continual improvement. When something goes wrong or the audit finds a gap — document it, fix the root cause, verify the fix. Close the PDCA loop.

NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0
Six functions, outcome-based security posture assessment
Framework

NIST CSF 2.0 — What Changed from 1.1

NIST CSF 2.0 (released February 2024) added a new sixth function — Govern — acknowledging that cybersecurity is a business risk decision, not just an IT problem. The other five functions (Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover) remain but were substantially updated. The framework now explicitly addresses supply chain risk and cloud security.

GV — Govern Establish context, strategy, and accountability Org context, risk strategy, roles, policy, supply chain risk oversight Draft security policy, define risk appetite, establish ISMS governance
ID — Identify Know your assets and risks Asset management, risk assessment, improvement planning Build asset inventory, conduct risk assessments, classify data
PR — Protect Implement safeguards Identity management, awareness training, data security, platform security Review control implementation, gap analysis, policy compliance checks
DE — Detect Find incidents fast Continuous monitoring, anomaly detection Define monitoring requirements, review SIEM coverage, KPI for MTTD
RS — Respond Act on incidents Incident management, analysis, mitigation, communication Maintain IR policy, review playbooks, test IR plan annually
RC — Recover Restore and learn Incident recovery, communication, recovery plan testing Ensure BCP/DRP alignment, track lessons learned, update controls post-incident

Risk Management — Theory to Practice
Methodologies, terminology, treatment options, and quantification
Core Skill

Risk = Threat × Vulnerability × Impact

A risk is the potential for a threat to exploit a vulnerability and cause harm to an asset. Risk management doesn't eliminate risk — it brings risk to an acceptable level (within risk appetite) at a justifiable cost.

Key terms: Asset — what has value (data, systems, reputation). Threat — what could harm it (attackers, fire, staff error). Vulnerability — weakness that enables the threat. Impact — harm caused if threat succeeds. Likelihood — probability it happens. Risk = Likelihood × Impact.

Qualitative Assessment
Rates likelihood and impact on scales (Low/Medium/High or 1–5). Fast, good for initial assessments. Subjective — requires expert judgment.
Quantitative Assessment
Uses numbers and money. SLE (Single Loss Expectancy) = Asset Value × Exposure Factor. ALE = SLE × ARO (Annual Rate of Occurrence). Harder, more defensible for budget decisions.
Treat — Mitigate
Implement a control to reduce likelihood or impact. Most common treatment. Must be cost-justified — control cost < risk cost.
Treat — Accept
Consciously accept the risk. Risk within appetite, or control cost exceeds risk cost. Must be formally documented and signed off by risk owner.
Treat — Transfer
Cyber insurance, contracts with SLAs and liability clauses, third-party processing agreements. Transfers financial impact, not the underlying risk.
Treat — Avoid
Stop doing the activity that creates the risk. Stop collecting unnecessary personal data. Don't deploy a vulnerable system. Eliminates risk entirely — sometimes the right call.

Threat Modelling — STRIDE & PASTA
Structured methods for identifying threats before they materialise
Technique

What is Threat Modelling?

Threat modelling is a structured exercise to identify threats to a system before it is built or deployed. It's GRC's contribution to the SDLC — shifting security left. The output is a prioritized list of threats and mitigations that feeds directly into risk register entries and security design decisions.

S — Spoofing
Pretending to be something you're not. Fake login, session hijack, IP spoofing. Violated property: Authentication.
T — Tampering
Modifying data or code without authorization. SQL injection changing records, man-in-the-middle altering packets. Integrity.
R — Repudiation
Denying having performed an action. No audit logs = can't prove who changed the database. Non-repudiation.
I — Info Disclosure
Exposing data to unauthorized parties. API returning PII in responses, error messages revealing stack traces. Confidentiality.
D — Denial of Service
Preventing legitimate users from accessing a service. DDoS, resource exhaustion attacks. Availability.
E — Elevation of Privilege
Gaining capabilities beyond what is authorized. Privilege escalation from user to admin. Authorization.

Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery
BCP vs DRP, key metrics, and writing a simple continuity plan
Planning

BCP vs DRP — The Crucial Distinction

BCP (Business Continuity Plan) is the broader plan: how does the business keep running during and after a disruptive event? It covers people, processes, suppliers, communications, alternative locations. DRP (Disaster Recovery Plan) is specifically about restoring IT systems and data after a disaster. DRP is a subset of BCP.

RTO — Recovery Time Objective
Maximum time a system can be down before unacceptable business impact. "Our payment system must be restored within 4 hours." Sets the deadline for your DRP.
RPO — Recovery Point Objective
Maximum data loss acceptable. "We can afford to lose 1 hour of transaction data." Determines backup frequency — RPO of 1 hour means backups at least hourly.
MTD — Maximum Tolerable Downtime
The absolute maximum time a business function can be unavailable before it threatens organizational survival. Always MTD > RTO — or your RTO is too ambitious.
WRT — Work Recovery Time
Time to restore and validate systems AFTER they're technically up. RTO + WRT = total time from disaster to normal operations. Often forgotten in DRP planning.

Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 🇮🇳
India's primary data protection law — every GRC analyst must know this
Indian Law ★

🇮🇳 Why DPDP is Critical for Indian GRC Careers

The DPDP Act 2023 is India's GDPR equivalent — passed in August 2023, with rules still being notified. Every organization processing digital personal data of Indian citizens is covered. Penalties reach ₹250 crore per violation. The DPDP Act is the most-asked Indian regulatory question in GRC interviews at Big 4, BFSI, and consulting firms.

Key Definitions — Know These Cold

Personal Data: Any data that can identify an individual — name, email, phone, Aadhaar, IP address, biometrics, location.

Data Fiduciary: The entity that determines the purpose and means of processing personal data — analogous to GDPR's "data controller." An Indian bank collecting customer KYC data is a data fiduciary.

Data Principal: The individual to whom the personal data belongs — the customer, employee, user. Has fundamental rights under the Act.

Data Processor: Processes data on behalf of a data fiduciary (e.g., cloud provider, payroll processor). Must follow fiduciary's instructions.

Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF): Entities designated by the government due to volume/sensitivity of data — banks, hospitals, large tech platforms. Additional obligations apply.

ObligationWhat It RequiresGRC Compliance Action
Consent Management Free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous consent before processing. Consent can be withdrawn. Build consent management system. Maintain consent records. Design "withdraw consent" mechanism on portal.
Purpose Limitation Collect only data necessary for declared purpose. Cannot use data for a different purpose without fresh consent. Data mapping exercise. Document purpose for each data element. Delete data when purpose fulfilled (storage limitation).
Data Principal Rights Right to access, right to correction, right to erasure, right to grievance redressal, right to nominate. Build request handling process. Respond within prescribed timeline. Designate Data Protection Officer (DPO) contact.
Security Safeguards Implement "reasonable security safeguards" to prevent breaches. Comparable to ISO 27001 implementation. Gap assess current controls against DPDP requirements. Implement encryption, access controls, audit logging.
Breach Notification Notify the Data Protection Board AND affected data principals of a personal data breach "without delay." Timeline TBD in rules. Write breach notification SOP. Draft notification templates. Test with tabletop exercise. Target: notify within 72 hours.
Children's Data Verifiable parental consent for processing data of children under 18. No behavioural tracking or targeted advertising. Age verification mechanism. Parental consent workflow. Audit ad targeting systems.
Cross-Border Transfer Transfer to countries notified by Government as permitted. Pending notification of allowed countries list. Map all cross-border data flows. Identify data processors outside India. Await government's "whitelist" notification.

CERT-In Directions 2022 🇮🇳
Mandatory cybersecurity reporting requirements for all Indian organisations
Indian Law ★

🇮🇳 CERT-In (Indian Computer Emergency Response Team) Directions — April 2022

Issued under Section 70B of the IT Act 2000. Mandatory for all service providers, intermediaries, data centres, cloud service providers and government organisations in India. Non-compliance can result in imprisonment up to 1 year and fines. These are not guidelines — they are legal obligations.

What is a Gap Analysis?

A gap analysis compares your organisation's current state (what you actually have) against a target state (what a standard or law requires). The "gap" is the delta — what's missing, partially implemented, or not working effectively. The output is a prioritized remediation roadmap.

Gap analysis is typically the first engagement a GRC consultant does with a new client. Everything else — risk register, SoA, policy work — follows from it.

Types of Audit Evidence

An auditor's job is to gather sufficient, appropriate evidence that a control either works or doesn't. Evidence comes in four types, in descending order of reliability: Observation (you saw it yourself — most reliable), Inspection (you reviewed the document/config), Re-performance (you ran the process yourself), Inquiry (someone told you — least reliable alone, must corroborate).

What is the Statement of Applicability (SoA)?

The SoA is a mandatory ISO 27001 document that lists all 93 Annex A controls and declares for each: Is it applicable to your ISMS? If not, why? If yes, is it implemented? It's the bridge between your risk treatment plan and the Annex A control set. An auditor will request the SoA on Day 1 of a certification audit.

A control can be excluded only if it is not applicable (e.g., a software company might exclude A.7.14 "secure disposal of equipment" if they are cloud-only). You cannot exclude a control simply because it's difficult or expensive — only because it genuinely doesn't apply to your context.
